A living meta-analysis of lignin-nanocellulose hybrid systems: interaction trends and performance thresholds

Esranur Kaya, Ronald Marquez, Marc Delgado-Aguilar

LEPAMAP-PRODIS Research Group, University of Girona. C/ Maria Aurèlia Capmany, 61 – 17003 Girona (Spain)
esranur.kaya@udg.edu, ronald.marquez@udg.edu, m.delgado@udg.edu

Abstract

Hybridizing nanocellulose with lignin offers a sustainable solution for biomass valorization, yet the development of these composites is primarily hindered by the structural variability and chemical heterogeneity of technical lignin. This results in poor interfacial compatibility, inhomogeneous dispersion, and difficulties in producing mechanically robust materials. To address these challenges and establish rational design principles, a meta-analysis was conducted by systematically extracting and normalizing quantitative data from studies on lignin–nanocellulose (L-NC) composites. The resulting database includes variables such as component ratios, synthesis routes, surface charge, and lignin molecular weight. The correlation of these variables reveals governing trends. First, in situ assembly methods successfully utilize the pre-existing fibrillar network as a spatial scaffold to confine lignin nanoparticles (LNPs) below 100 nm, preventing macroscopic coalescence. Second, normalization of formulations reveals a distinct structural divergence in performance thresholds based on material architecture. For dense 2D biocomposite films, a low-to-moderate L:NC mass ratio (within a 2.5:97.5 to 15:85 range) is required to act as a macromolecular plasticizer and physical nano-spacer, whereas exceeding a 20:80 ratio induces macroscopic agglomeration and mechanical failure. Conversely, highly porous 3D frameworks (e.g., aerogels and membranes) accommodate higher L:NC ratios (averaging ~33:67) to provide sufficient active sites for functional adsorption and conductivity. Third, the electrostatics of the template govern nanoscale assembly. Mildly negative cellulose nanofibrils yield lignin nanoparticle assemblies, while positively charged templates drive electrostatic adsorption and binding. Fourth, overcoming water susceptibility requires sacrificial networks via dynamic ionic or covalent cross-linking, which neutralizes swelling and yields high wet tensile strengths. Fifth, for extreme barrier applications, esterifying lignin creates thermally triggered sealants that induce pore collapse during hot-pressing, reducing water vapor transmission. Finally, bioactive properties, including UV-shielding and antioxidant capacity, scale linearly with lignin content independent of mechanical limits. An interactive living meta-analysis accessible with a QR code was developed. This work provides a data-driven framework for designing high-performance lignocellulosic biocomposites and supports their use in applications ranging from sustainable packaging to flexible electronics.
